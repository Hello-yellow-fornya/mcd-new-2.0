## What like-for-like means

[Like-for-like](/non-fault-accident-courtesy-car/) does not mean an identical car. It means a vehicle that is equivalent in size, body type, and practical use. If you drive a medium SUV, you should receive a medium SUV and not a city hatchback. If you drive a van for work, you should receive a van of equivalent carrying capacity. The principle is that you should not be materially disadvantaged while someone else’s mistake is being resolved.

<Callout lead="Not identical, equivalent">On a non-fault claim, like-for-like means the replacement serves the same practical purpose. A vehicle significantly smaller or less capable than the one you have lost does not meet the standard.</Callout>

## What you are not automatically entitled to

- A higher-specification vehicle than the one you lost
- A brand-new vehicle; the replacement is a hire vehicle, not a settlement payment
- An indefinite hire period, the replacement covers the reasonable time your vehicle is off the road

## Who provides the replacement and who pays

Through us, the replacement vehicle is arranged on a [credit hire](/credit-hire/) basis. We source the vehicle, and the cost of the hire is recovered from the at-fault driver’s insurer. You do not pay for it, the other side does.

This is different from a courtesy car provided through your own insurer. A courtesy car is typically a standard small hire vehicle from a fixed category, and is not guaranteed to be equivalent to the vehicle you have lost. The like-for-like entitlement applies on a non-fault claim, when you claim through the at-fault driver’s insurer.

## How long you can keep the replacement

The hire period covers the repair period if your vehicle is repairable, plus a reasonable additional period to source a replacement if the vehicle is [written off](/car-written-off-not-my-fault/). The at-fault insurer can challenge hire periods they consider excessive, and we manage those disputes on your behalf.
