## Who is at fault?

A legally parked car cannot cause an accident. If another vehicle drove into it, the driver of that vehicle is liable for the damage. Their insurer is responsible for putting it right.

The only exception is if your car was parked illegally such as on a yellow line, blocking a junction, or in a way that created a hazard. In those cases, a court might find you contributed to the accident. If you were parked lawfully, liability is clear.

<Callout lead="The starting point">If your car was legally parked and another vehicle drove into it, the other driver is at fault. Their insurer pays for the damage, not yours.</Callout>

## If they drove off without stopping

If the driver who hit your car left without leaving details, the claim becomes more complicated but it is not lost. Car parks almost always have CCTV, and footage is typically kept for at least 30 days. Act quickly.

If the driver can be identified from CCTV or witness accounts, you can claim from their insurer in the normal way. If they cannot be traced, the [Motor Insurers' Bureau](/advice/what-to-do-if-hit-by-uninsured-driver/) handles claims against untraced drivers for property damage, though conditions apply.

## What to collect at the scene

If the driver stopped, collect as much of the following as possible.

- Their full name and home address
- Vehicle registration number
- Name of their insurance company and policy number
- Their phone number
- Photographs of both vehicles, the damage, and the position in the car park or road
- Names and contact numbers of any witnesses

## If no details were left

Report the incident to the police, they can trace the registered keeper from the plate number. Note the exact time and location of the incident. If it happened in a managed car park, contact the operator: they control the CCTV and may be able to identify the driver or provide footage directly.

## Who pays for the repair?

If the other driver is identified and insured, their insurer pays for the repair. You are not required to go through your own policy, [pay your excess](/do-i-have-to-pay-excess-if-not-my-fault/), or affect your [no-claims bonus](/will-a-non-fault-accident-affect-my-insurance/).

If you contact the at-fault driver’s insurer directly, they will handle the claim on behalf of their policyholder.
