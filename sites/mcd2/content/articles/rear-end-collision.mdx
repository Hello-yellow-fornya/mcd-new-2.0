## Who is usually at fault?

The driver who runs into the back of another vehicle has, in most cases, failed to maintain a safe following distance or failed to react to traffic ahead in time. The Highway Code requires every driver to be able to stop safely within the distance they can see to be clear.

This makes [rear-end collisions](/how-to-prove-fault/rear-end-collision/) one of the more consistent fault positions in motor claims. The physical evidence, such as where the damage sits on each vehicle, typically supports the account of the driver who was hit.

<Callout lead="The starting point">In most [rear-end collisions](/how-to-prove-fault/rear-end-collision/), the driver behind is at fault. The exceptions are limited, and they require evidence to support them.</Callout>

## When it isn’t that simple

There are circumstances where the position is less straightforward:

- The front driver reversed into the vehicle behind
- The front driver braked abruptly without cause, for example, to make a turn without signalling
- A vehicle cut sharply in front of the rear driver immediately before the collision, leaving no time to react
- A third vehicle pushed the rear driver into the car in front

## What evidence helps

In most rear-end cases, the position of the damage is the clearest evidence. Where fault is disputed, additional evidence strengthens the position:

- Dash cam footage: dash cams and non-fault accidents
- Photographs of both vehicles and their final positions
- Witness contact details
- CCTV from nearby buildings or businesses
