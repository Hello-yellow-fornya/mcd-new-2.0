## The two routes after a non-fault accident

When another driver causes an accident, you have two ways to claim. The route you choose determines whether you pay an excess, whether your no-claims record is affected, and what kind of replacement vehicle you get.

### Route one: claim on your own policy

You notify your insurer, pay your excess, and they arrange the repair. Your insurer then pursues the at-fault driver’s insurer to recover that money. If they succeed, your excess is refunded and the claim may be recorded as non-fault. That process can take months, and there is no guarantee the recovery will be made in full. Your no-claims record may be affected while the claim remains open.

### Route two: claim from the driver who hit you

When someone else caused the accident, their insurer is liable for your losses. You do not need to use your own policy at all. On a non-fault claim, an accident management company acts on your behalf to recover everything from the at-fault insurer: the repair or write-off value, a like-for-like replacement car, and any out-of-pocket costs. Nothing goes through your policy. [How a third party insurance claim works](/third-party-insurance-claim/).

## Why the difference exists

A claims department employed by your insurer works in your insurer’s interests. That is not criticism, it is just what they are paid to do. When you claim on your own policy, your insurer manages the cost of your claim. When you use an accident management company, we work for you and recover our costs from the at-fault insurer. The financial relationship is different, and so is the outcome. [How accident management works](/how-accident-management-works/).

## When your insurer is the right call

There are situations where claiming on your own policy is the correct route. We will tell you if your situation is one of them on the first call.

- The accident was your fault, or partly your fault. If liability falls on you or is genuinely shared, the at-fault driver’s insurer will not meet your costs. Your own policy is the right starting point.
- The other driver was uninsured or drove off. If the other driver cannot be traced or had no valid insurance, there is no insurer to claim from. A separate process applies. [What to do if hit by an uninsured driver](/advice/what-to-do-if-hit-by-uninsured-driver/).
- Fault is genuinely disputed and there is no evidence. If both accounts conflict and no independent evidence exists, the position may not support a third-party claim. We will assess this on the first call, before you sign anything.

## How to choose

Most non-fault accidents suit the third-party route: another driver was at fault, they hold valid motor insurance, and their insurer meets your costs. That is what funds the replacement car and covers our fees, so the claim costs you nothing. [How credit hire works](/credit-hire/).

Where the accident does not fit that pattern, your own policy is the better route, and the list above sets out when. One call settles it. We look at what happened, tell you which route applies, and you decide from there.
