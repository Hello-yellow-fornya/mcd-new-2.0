## What counts as a non-fault accident?

A non-fault accident is one where another driver was responsible for the collision. It does not mean there are no arguments about what happened. It means that when the facts are assessed, you were not the cause.

The term matters because it determines who pays. If the accident was not your fault, the other driver’s insurer is liable for your losses. You do not have to go through your own policy.

### Common non-fault situations

- You were hit from behind: [rear-end collisions](/how-to-prove-fault/rear-end-collision/)
- You were parked or stationary: [someone hit my parked car](/how-to-prove-fault/car-park-accidents/parked-car/)
- They pulled out or changed lanes into you: [side-impact collisions](/how-to-prove-fault/side-impact-collision/)

## Who pays after a non-fault accident

When another driver causes an accident, they are liable for your losses. Their insurer stands behind that liability. You can recover the cost of repairs, a replacement vehicle, and other reasonable out-of-pocket expenses from them directly, without your own policy being involved.

The at-fault driver’s insurer may contact you quickly after the accident. You are not obliged to deal with them. If you have already called us, tell them we are handling it.

## What happens to your excess, no-claims and premium

If we handle the claim as a non-fault case, nothing goes through your own policy. Your excess is not triggered. Your no-claims record is not affected.

Whether a non-fault accident affects your renewal premium is a question that depends on your insurer and your policy terms with them.

## A car while yours is fixed

While your car is off the road after a non-fault accident, you are entitled to a replacement vehicle. Through us, that is a like-for-like car delivered to your address. The cost is recovered from the at-fault driver’s insurer.

## The catch

This route works when fault is clear and the other driver is insured. If that is not the case, we will tell you on the first call.
