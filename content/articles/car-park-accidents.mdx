## Who is usually at fault?

In most car park collisions, the driver who was moving when the collision occurred is at fault, particularly where they struck a stationary vehicle. A driver reversing out of a bay has the greatest obligation to check the lane is clear before moving.

Car park accidents are complicated by the absence of formal right-of-way rules. There are no traffic signals governing most car park lanes, and no Highway Code rule that covers every situation precisely.

<Callout lead="The starting point">The driver who was moving when the collision occurred is usually at fault. Where both cars were moving, fault depends on the specific circumstances and what evidence is available.</Callout>

## When it isn’t that simple

Car park accidents become less clear-cut when:

- Both cars were reversing at the same time
- A driver on a main through-lane was moving faster than appropriate for a car park environment
- Parking bay lines were unclear or poorly marked
- A vehicle was parked partly outside its bay

## If your car was parked

If your car was parked and stationary when it was hit, fault ordinarily falls on the driver who struck it. This applies whether the other driver was reversing out of a bay, travelling along a lane, or moving in any other way.

## If both cars were moving

Where both cars were moving when the collision occurred, the analysis considers which driver had priority, whether either driver was travelling in a way inconsistent with a car park setting, and what each could reasonably have seen before impact. Dash cam footage and witness accounts are particularly useful in these cases.

## What evidence helps

Car parks often have good CCTV coverage, which is worth requesting promptly. Other useful evidence includes:

- Dash cam footage: dash cams and non-fault accidents
- Photographs of both vehicles, the bay markings and the lane layout
- Witness contact details
