## What is an accident management company?

An accident management company runs a non-fault claim from start to finish and recovers the cost from the driver who caused the accident, through their insurer. That is a different route from claiming on your own policy. Your insurer’s claims department works for your insurer. We work for you.

In practice it means one phone call. We check who was at fault and whether they are insured, tell you straight away if your claim is one we can take on, and then handle the rest: recovering your car if it cannot be driven, arranging the repair, delivering a replacement car to your door and dealing with the other driver’s insurer until the claim is settled.

## Accident management vs claiming on your insurance

Both routes get your car fixed. The difference is who pays and what it does to your policy. Claim on your own insurance and you pay your excess, a claim goes on your record and the courtesy car, if you get one at all, is whatever your policy says. Claim from the driver who hit you and none of that applies. [See the full comparison](/accident-management-vs-insurance/).

## When your insurer is the right call

We can only help when someone else was at fault and they were insured. If the accident was your fault, if fault is genuinely shared, or if the other driver cannot be traced and was not insured, your own policy is the right route, and we will say so on the first call rather than take on a claim we cannot recover. [Hit by an uninsured driver](/advice/what-to-do-if-hit-by-uninsured-driver/) explains the exception for untraced and uninsured drivers.

## Who pays, and the catch

The at-fault driver’s insurer pays: the recovery, the repair, the replacement car and our fees. The replacement car is arranged on credit hire, which is how you get a car without paying for it while the claim is open. [How credit hire works](/credit-hire/). There is one thing to know before you sign anything, and we would rather you heard it from us.
