## What is a third party insurance claim?

In a road traffic accident, the driver who caused the collision is legally liable for the losses of anyone they hit. Their motor insurance policy is there to meet that liability. A third party claim is a claim made directly against that policy.

The term "third party" describes your position in the at-fault driver’s insurance contract. Their insurer is the first party, their policyholder is the second, and you as the injured party are the third.

<Callout lead="The short version">When another driver causes a crash, they’re liable for your losses and their insurer stands behind that. You can claim from them directly, nothing has to go through your own policy.</Callout>

## What you can claim for

From the at-fault driver’s insurer, you can claim for:

- Repairs to your car, or its market value if it is written off
- A replacement vehicle while your car is off the road
- Recovery and storage costs
- Out-of-pocket expenses that resulted directly from the accident

## Why the other driver’s insurer will ring you first

After an accident, the at-fault insurer is often notified quickly by their own policyholder. They may call you directly, sometimes within hours. The purpose of that call is to manage the claim on their terms, which typically means a lower settlement, their choice of repairer, and a basic replacement car rather than a like-for-like one.

You are not obliged to deal with them. If we are already handling your claim, tell them to contact us.

## The catch

We can only pursue a third party claim when fault is established and the other driver holds valid insurance. If those conditions are not met, the route is not available. We will tell you on the first call.
