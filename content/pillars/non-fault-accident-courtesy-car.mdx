## What you’re entitled to after a non-fault accident

When another driver is responsible for an accident, they are liable for restoring you to the position you were in before it happened. That includes the loss of use of your vehicle. You are entitled to a replacement car for as long as your car is reasonably off the road.

That entitlement comes from the law of damages, not from your own insurance policy. It exists whether or not your own policy includes a courtesy car benefit.

## How quickly the car arrives

Once your claim is confirmed, we arrange delivery of a replacement vehicle to your address. We will give you a realistic timeframe when we speak to you.

## If your car is written off

If your car is declared a total loss, the repair stops and the at-fault insurer pays your car’s market value instead. The replacement vehicle stays with you until that settlement is agreed and the money is in your account. You are not left without transport while the total loss process runs.

[Car written off, not my fault](/car-written-off-not-my-fault/) · [Total loss](/total-loss/).

## Who pays for the car

The replacement vehicle is arranged on credit hire. You take the car without paying for it upfront. The hire cost is recovered from the at-fault driver’s insurer as part of your claim. You do not receive a bill.

[How credit hire works](/credit-hire/).

## The catch

This applies when another driver was at fault and holds valid motor insurance. If fault is unclear or the other driver was uninsured, credit hire is not available. We will tell you on the first call.
