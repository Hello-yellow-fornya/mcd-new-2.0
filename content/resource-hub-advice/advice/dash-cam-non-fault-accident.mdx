## What dash cam footage can prove

- The direction and speed of each vehicle in the moments before impact
- Which vehicle moved into the other’s path
- The position of both vehicles in the road
- Whether traffic signals, signs or road markings were observed
- What was said at the scene, if the microphone was active

## What footage cannot prove alone

Footage shows what happened, not necessarily who was legally liable. In complex scenarios we see such as shared-fault situations or cases where speed is in dispute then footage is strong evidence, but not automatic proof. In most straightforward non-fault cases, clear footage resolves the dispute quickly and without further argument.

## What to do with your footage immediately after an accident

- Do not overwrite or reformat the memory card. Remove it and back it up as soon as possible.
- Note the date and time settings on your camera. If they are incorrect, note the offset.
- Do not share the footage with the other driver or their insurer without taking advice first.
- Tell us you have footage when you call. We will advise how to use it.

<Callout lead="Back it up now">Dash cam footage loops and overwrites automatically. If you do not remove and back up the memory card quickly, the accident footage may be gone. Do this before you do anything else.</Callout>

## If the other driver also has a dash cam

The other driver’s footage may also be relevant to your claim and they may be required to disclose it in legal proceedings. If you know the other driver had a camera fitted, tell us. In many cases, the at-fault driver’s own footage is the clearest evidence available.

## If you do not have a dash cam

Most modern car parks and many road junctions are covered by fixed CCTV. Footage is typically retained for around 30 days. If you report the accident and request preservation of the footage promptly, it can usually be obtained. Local authority and Highways England cameras are the most useful on public roads.
